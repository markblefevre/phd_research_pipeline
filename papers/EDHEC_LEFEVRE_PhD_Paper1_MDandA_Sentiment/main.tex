\begin{abstract}
\noindent 
We examine whether disclosure sentiment predicts market reactions to Japanese corporate filings and whether an LLM-based sentiment measure provides incremental explanatory power beyond a domain-specific lexical measure in this setting. Using MD\&A sections from annual securities reports filed by Nikkei 225 firms, we compare a finance-specific Japanese adaptation of the Loughran–McDonald dictionary with a GPT-based document-level sentiment score. Lexicon-based sentiment is more consistently associated with abnormal returns, while the GPT coefficient attenuates and becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero when both measures are included. The results highlight the continued importance of domain-specific language when measuring disclosure tone in structured non-English regulatory filings.
